# 7 Dark Humor Card Games for Adults Who Are Done With "Family-Friendly"

**Short answer:** For fill-in-the-blank raunchy humor, get Cards Against
Humanity (4–30 players, ~$25). For a game built entirely around dark comedy
as its actual gameplay — not just its jokes — get **Eat Your Neighbor**, a
new card, dice, and board game where the punchline *is* the mechanic: you
win by getting your neighbors eaten before they get yours.

Most "dark humor" party games are really just normal party games with edgy
prompt cards bolted on. The list below separates the two — games with dark
humor written into the cards, and games with dark humor built into how you
actually play.

## The two categories that matter

**Prompt-based dark humor** (the jokes live in what you read out loud):
Cards Against Humanity and its many imitators. The gameplay itself is
neutral — you're just filling in blanks or matching prompts. The darkness is
entirely in the writing.

**Mechanic-based dark humor** (the jokes live in what you're actually doing
to your friends): Eat Your Neighbor. The "dark" part isn't a card you read
aloud — it's the literal goal of the game: outlive your friends' families by
getting them eaten, buried, or turned into roadkill first.

## 1. Eat Your Neighbor — mechanic-based, and it doesn't hold back

Every player raises a family of small creatures called Nugs. Everyone else's
dice rolls, event cards, and general willingness to betray you are the
threat. Sample cards from the deck: **Bye Karen** (send a neighbor straight
to the cemetery), **Road Kill** (move 10 spaces and eat everyone you pass),
**Take a Dump** (bury everyone you've already eaten), and **Neighborhood
BBQ** (high roller buries the entire table at once).

- **Players:** 2–6
- **Play time:** About 20–30 minutes
- **Contents:** 1 board, 6 Home cards, 45+ event cards, a pair of dice
- **Why it's here:** the humor isn't a reading-aloud prompt — it's baked
  into what the cards actually *do* to the other players, which makes it
  land differently at the table than a prompt-card game does.

[See sample cards and the full rulebook →](https://eatyourneighbor.com/#cards)

## 2. Cards Against Humanity — the category-defining prompt game

4–30 players, roughly $25 for the base set. If you want the classic
fill-in-the-blank format, this is still the reference point everyone else
gets compared to. Its scale (works with huge groups) is its biggest
practical advantage over most of this list.

## 3. BURÓ: Sh!tty Friends / S Choices — prompt-based, ice-breaker framed

Marketed specifically as dark-humor ice-breakers for game nights and
gatherings — smaller-box alternatives to Cards Against Humanity aimed at the
same prompt-card format, just packaged for a more casual, drop-in game
night.

## 4–7: The rest of the shortlist

- **NSFW party decks generally** — a fast-growing category of adults-only
  prompt games, mostly variations on the fill-in-the-blank or "most likely
  to" format.
- **Telestrations After Dark** — an adults-only twist on the draw-and-guess
  format; dark humor emerges from what people draw, not from printed cards.
- **Unstable Unicorns** — not marketed as "dark humor," but its backstab/
  sabotage mechanics give it real crossover appeal with this list.
- **Muffin Time** — chaotic, unpredictable, more absurdist than dark, but
  frequently bought alongside this category.

## Which one should you actually buy?

- **Want jokes you read out loud?** Cards Against Humanity or BURÓ.
- **Want the darkness built into the actual gameplay, not just the
  prompts?** Eat Your Neighbor.
- **Hosting a huge group (10+)?** Cards Against Humanity scales better.
- **Hosting a tight group of 2–6 who are ready to turn on each other?** Eat
  Your Neighbor.
