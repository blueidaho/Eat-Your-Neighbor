# Most Anticipated Tabletop Games of 2026 (Including One You Can't Buy Yet)

**Short answer:** 2026's biggest tabletop stories so far include the
record-breaking Cyberpunk Trading Card Game (over $26.9M raised, the most
funded tabletop game in Kickstarter history), Discworld: Adventures in
Ankh-Morpork (over $3.2M raised, shipping to backers in Q2), and a wave of
new party games chasing the post-Exploding-Kittens crowd — including
**Eat Your Neighbor**, which hasn't launched its Kickstarter yet but is
already building a pre-launch waitlist.

## The big funded stories of 2026 so far

**Cyberpunk Trading Card Game** — closed April 17, 2026, with $26,953,978
raised from 47,676 backers, making it the most funded TCG and the most
funded tabletop game in Kickstarter history to date.

**Discworld: Adventures in Ankh-Morpork** — from Modiphius, raised over
$3.2 million and began shipping to backers in Q2 2026.

**Moytura** — a tarot-sized card game pairing strong artwork with
asymmetric faction abilities and tight tactical combat, drawing comparisons
to a compact, heavily-themed euro game.

## New party and card games to watch

**All In: Predictions** — designer Phil Walker-Harding (known for Sushi
Go) brings a gamer-focused twist on poker-hand manipulation, letting
players discard cards for powers and reclaim cards others discarded.

**Rock Hard 1977: Ear Candy** — combines music trivia with resource
management, positioned as a potential party-game breakout of the season.

**Eat Your Neighbor** — hasn't launched its Kickstarter campaign yet, which
makes it the wildcard on this list. It's a card, dice, and board game for
2–6 players about betrayal, backyard BBQs, and getting your neighbors'
"Nugs" eaten, run over, or buried before they get yours — built for the
crowd that's already worn out Exploding Kittens and Cards Against Humanity.
No funding numbers to report yet, because there's no campaign yet — but
that also means early sign-ups get first access to backer pricing the
moment it opens.
[Get notified at launch →](https://eatyourneighbor.com)

## Why watch a game that hasn't even launched?

Every game above already has funding numbers because they've already run
their campaigns. The actual edge in backing crowdfunded games is getting in
*before* the numbers exist — when early-bird tiers are still open and
pricing hasn't climbed. If you back projects specifically to catch that
window, a pre-launch signup list is the highest-leverage five seconds you
can spend on a game you're curious about.
