# Funny Gifts for New Neighbors (That Aren't a Bundt Cake)

**Short answer:** Skip the candle. The standard new-neighbor welcome kit
— a mug, a candle, a "Best Neighbor" sign — is nice but forgettable.
A game you can actually play together the first time you hang out is
the gift that turns "the people next door" into "the people we invite
over." **Eat Your Neighbor** is the obvious pick for the pun alone, but
it also happens to work as an actual icebreaker: 20-30 minutes, easy to
teach, and it's a lot faster to bond over shared betrayal than over a
candle neither of you will burn.

Every "gifts for new neighbors" list online is some combination of a
mug, a candle, a plate of cookies, and a fire pit. None of it is bad —
it's just the same list everyone else gets too. Here's a shorter list of
gifts that are actually funny, not just "nice," starting with the one
that's basically a pun made physical.

## 1. Eat Your Neighbor — for the neighbor with a sense of humor

A card, dice, and board game where every player raises a family of
small creatures called Nugs, and the whole table tries to get someone
else's family eaten, buried, or run over first. Handing this to a new
neighbor as a "welcome to the street" gift is either going to get a
big laugh or immediately tell you everything you need to know about
whether you're going to get along. 2–6 players, 20–30 minutes, easy to
teach on the spot.
[See sample cards →](https://eatyourneighbor.com/#cards)

## 2. A "Best Neighbor Ever" candle (the control group)

The default gift for a reason — low effort, universally fine, and
nobody's ever offended by a candle. Just don't expect it to start a
friendship on its own.

## 3. A local coffee or spice blend

Practical, consumable, and it doesn't ask them to do anything with it
beyond enjoy it once. A safe pick if you don't know their sense of humor
yet.

## 4. A compact tabletop fire pit

A genuinely good "let's hang out on the porch" gift if you're hoping
to actually spend time together — more of an investment than most
welcome gifts, so it signals you're planning on this being a real
friendship, not a one-time gesture.

## 5. Sparklers or a six-pack

Low-effort, zero-pressure, and it doubles as an invitation: "come use
these with us" is an easier ask than "come over sometime."

## Which one should you actually give?

- **Want to actually start a friendship, not just say hello?** Eat Your Neighbor — you'll know within one game whether you like them.
- **Don't know them at all yet, want something safe?** Candle or coffee.
- **Hoping this turns into regular porch hangouts?** Fire pit.
- **Want a zero-pressure "come hang out" excuse?** Sparklers or a six-pack.
