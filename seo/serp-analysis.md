# SERP Analysis — Top 3 Priority Keywords

## 1. "Games like Exploding Kittens"

**Who ranks:** Bustle, GigaBrain, EditionCards, Find Game Like, Very Special
Games, Great Board Games Blog, TheGamer, GameNightGods, Don't Eat the Meeples
— all independent blogs/content sites, zero major outlets (no NYT Wirecutter,
no IGN). Games most commonly cited as "alternatives": Unstable Unicorns,
Throw Throw Burrito, Taco Cat Goat Cheese Pizza, Muffin Time, Here to Slay,
Bears vs Babies, Love Letter, Coup.

**Winning format:** Numbered listicle ("7 Best...", "10 Games Like...").
Each entry = game name + 2-3 sentence pitch on what makes it similar/different
+ usually a buy link. No entry is more than a short paragraph — these are
scannable, not essay-style reviews.

**Why it's winnable:** No dominant, deeply authoritative single source (unlike
e.g. "best laptops" style commodity terms). Domain authority bar is low —
these are mostly niche blogs, not major publishers. None currently include
Eat Your Neighbor (it didn't exist when they were written), so there's no
incumbent defending the specific "should Eat Your Neighbor be on this list"
slot.

**AI-answer angle:** An LLM-style answer to "games like Exploding Kittens"
will synthesize across these same listicles. The way to get cited is to *be
citable*: publish our own comprehensive, well-structured version of this list
that explicitly includes and contextualizes Eat Your Neighbor alongside the
already-established names (not just a promotional page), with clear game
descriptions an LLM can lift directly.

## 2. "Dark humor card games for adults"

**Who ranks:** coopboardgames.com, thegamersmag.com, lost-boy-entertainment.com,
qpmarketnetwork.com — smaller, less established domains than Cluster 1's
competitors. Games cited: Cards Against Humanity, BURÓ (Sh!tty Friends, S
Choices), various NSFW party decks.

**Winning format:** Same listicle pattern, slightly more "shock value" framed
copy in headers/intros than Cluster 1 (these pages lean into the NSFW/edgy
angle explicitly in their titles and hooks).

**Why it's winnable:** Lowest domain authority of all three clusters — the
easiest to outrank with a genuinely well-written, well-structured page.
This is also the most authentic fit for Eat Your Neighbor's actual brand
voice (dark, absurd, comic-book style), so the content will read as
non-generic by default rather than forced.

**AI-answer angle:** Same synthesis pattern as Cluster 1 — be the clearly
labeled, well-organized list an LLM can extract from directly, and be sure to
include Eat Your Neighbor with a description that clearly differentiates it
(cannibalism/neighbor-betrayal theme vs. generic "shock value" framing most
competitors use).

## 3. "New party card games on Kickstarter 2026"

**Who ranks:** Kickstarter's own discover/project pages, BoardGameGeek
geeklists (community-curated, high trust), Bitewing Games and SinfulShadows
"most anticipated" editorial roundups, EverythingBoardGames.

**Winning format:** Mixed — some roundup listicles (Bitewing/SinfulShadows
style), but a large share of the real traffic/authority here is Kickstarter's
own platform pages and BGG community lists, which we can't outrank directly
with a blog post. The realistic play is a **"What to expect" / campaign
preview page** optimized as a standalone resource (not a listicle imitating
the roundup sites), built to be genuinely useful to someone deciding whether
to back — plus, this is content we can promote directly to get picked up
*by* those existing roundup pages and BGG geeklists (a distinct, valid path
to citation/backlinks separate from ranking #1 ourselves on Google).

**Why it's winnable (differently than 1 & 2):** We won't outrank Kickstarter
itself or BGG on raw authority, but this keyword cluster's real value is as
a **conversion and outreach asset** — a page worth linking to from BGG
geeklists, Reddit r/tabletopgamedeals, and gift-guide writers covering 2026
Kickstarter launches, not a page we expect to rank #1 on its own.

**AI-answer angle:** Structured FAQ content ("What is Eat Your Neighbor?",
"When does it launch on Kickstarter?", "How many players?") is the most
likely format to get pulled into an AI Overview for "eat your neighbor
kickstarter" / "new kickstarter card games 2026" once the campaign is live
and has even minimal citation activity.
