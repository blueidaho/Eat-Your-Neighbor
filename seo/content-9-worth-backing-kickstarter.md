# How to Tell Which Kickstarter Board Games Are Actually Worth Backing

**Short answer:** Don't evaluate a Kickstarter campaign like a finished
product — it's a promise, not a purchase. The campaigns worth backing can
explain their mechanics in specific, operational terms (what happens on
your turn, what happens when you fall behind), not just mood-board
adjectives and atmospheric art. Everything else is a coin flip.

Backing a board game on Kickstarter is different from buying one off a
shelf. There's no returns process, no guarantee of delivery, and
sometimes a year-plus wait between pledging and getting a box. That's not
a reason to avoid crowdfunding — some of the best games of the last decade
started this way — but it is a reason to actually check a few things
before you pledge.

## 1. Can they explain the game without adjectives?

The single biggest tell: does the campaign page describe exactly how a
turn works, what the tension loop is in the final round, and what happens
when a player falls behind — or does it lean entirely on words like
"epic," "immersive," and "unforgettable"? A campaign that can't describe
its own mechanics in plain, specific language is often still looking for a
game to attach to its concept.

## 2. Is there real gameplay footage, not just concept art?

Concept art and mockups are cheap. Actual footage of real people playing
actual prototypes — even rough ones — is the strongest signal that a
playable game exists today, not just an idea of one.

## 3. Does the creator show up, or hide behind the campaign page?

Campaigns run by someone who answers comments, posts real updates, and
is visibly present tend to ship. Campaigns that go quiet after the funding
goal is hit are the ones that generate the most "still waiting" threads a
year later.
