# 9 Backyard BBQ Party Games for Adults (Ranked by How Fast They Get Chaotic)

**Short answer:** Lawn games like cornhole and Spikeball are the obvious
warm-up — low effort, everyone already knows the rules. But once the
food's on plates and people are sitting down, you need something that
travels to the table. **Eat Your Neighbor** was built for exactly that
moment: a card, dice, and board game about betrayal, backyard BBQs, and
burying the neighbors you used to wave to — it's not just BBQ-adjacent,
it's set at one.

Most "BBQ games for adults" lists are 90% lawn games and one
afterthought card game at the bottom. That's backwards — lawn games
need daylight, space, and people willing to stand up again after
they've already found a lawn chair. Here's a full lineup, sorted by
what actually works once the sun starts going down.

## Quick comparison

| Game | Type | Space Needed | Works After Dark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornhole | Lawn | Large | No (unless lit) |
| Spikeball | Lawn | Medium | No |
| Ladder toss | Lawn | Medium | No |
| Giant Jenga | Yard/patio | Small | With good lighting |
| **Eat Your Neighbor** | **Table/card** | **None** | **Yes** |
| Cards Against Humanity | Table/card | None | Yes |
| Beer pong / yard pong | Lawn/patio | Medium | With lighting |
| Horseshoes | Lawn | Large | No |
| Trivia | Table | None | Yes |

## 1. Cornhole — the default for a reason

Bring a set, set it up on the grass, and people will play it
unprompted before you even announce the party's started. Works for
almost any group size, doesn't need explanation, and keeps people
moving while the grill's still going.

## 2. Spikeball — for the group that wants to actually sweat

Fast, physical, 2-on-2. Best for a group that's already active and
wants something with real intensity, not just a lawn game to half-pay
attention to.

## 3. Ladder toss — the low-key alternative to cornhole

Same "bring it, set it up, no rules explanation needed" energy as
cornhole, just with bolas and a ladder frame instead of bags and a
board. Good second lawn game if you already own cornhole and want
variety.

## 4. Giant Jenga — the transition game

Works on a patio, doesn't need full yard space, and it's the natural
bridge between "everyone's standing around the grill" and "everyone's
ready to sit down." Loud when it falls, which is half the appeal.

## 5. Eat Your Neighbor — for once people sit down at the table

This is the one built for the specific moment lawn games can't cover:
plates are out, people are sitting, and standing back up for cornhole
isn't happening. Every player raises a family of small creatures
called Nugs, and the whole table tries to get someone else's family
eaten, buried, or run over first. 2&ndash;6 players, 20&ndash;30
minutes, plays just as well after dark as it does at 2pm, and it's
already thematically at the BBQ &mdash; sample cards include
**Neighborhood BBQ** itself, where the high roller buries the entire
table at once.
[See sample cards &rarr;](https://eatyourneighbor.com/#cards)

## 6. Cards Against Humanity — the other default table game

Everyone already half-owns this. Good for bigger tables (up to 20
players with expansions), though the humor lives entirely in the
prompt cards rather than in anything players are actually doing to
each other.

## 7. Yard pong — beer pong, scaled up for outdoor play

Extra-large buckets instead of cups, played on the lawn instead of a
folding table. Needs some lighting once the sun goes down, but it's a
reliable crowd favorite for groups that like beer pong's format
without the cramped indoor setup.

## 8. Horseshoes — the classic that never left

Needs the most space on this list and doesn't work once it's dark, but
if you've already got a pit set up in the yard, it's an easy,
zero-explanation option for guests trickling in early.

## 9. Trivia — the low-effort fallback

Works with zero setup if you already know your group's interests.
Split into teams to keep energy up; best used as a filler activity
between the louder games, not the main event.

## Which one should you actually bring?

- **Want something for while the grill's going?** Cornhole or Spikeball.
- **Want a bridge activity as people start sitting down?** Giant Jenga.
- **Want the game built for the table, after dark, no reset needed?** Eat Your Neighbor.
- **Big group, zero prep time?** Cards Against Humanity or trivia.
