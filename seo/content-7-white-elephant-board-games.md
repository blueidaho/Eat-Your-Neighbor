# Best White Elephant Gifts for People Who Love Board Games

**Short answer:** White elephant gifts should be funny, cheap enough to
not feel like a real present, and good enough that people actually fight
over them. A boxed party game hits all three — and **Eat Your Neighbor**
is built specifically to be the gift someone steals three times.

Most white elephant gift exchanges have a price cap around $25 and a
simple rule: bring something people will actually want to steal.
Gag gifts are funny once. A genuinely good, funny party game gets stolen
repeatedly, which is the entire point of the game-within-the-game.

## What actually makes a good white elephant gift

The best white elephant board game gifts share three things: they're
funny to unwrap in front of a group, they're cheap enough to not feel like
you spent real money, and they're good enough that the next person in line
actually wants to steal it instead of taking a new box.

## Our pick: Eat Your Neighbor

A card, dice, and board game for 2–6 players where everyone raises a
family of small creatures called Nugs, and the whole table is trying to
get yours eaten, buried, or run over first. It plays in 20–30 minutes,
which matters at a party where people are also eating, drinking, and doing

## Other white elephant traditions worth stealing (pun intended)

If you want to make the exchange itself funnier, not just the gifts:

- **White Elephant Bingo** — players mark off a card as things happen
  during the exchange (a new gift gets opened, a steal happens, someone
  argues about the rules).
- **The Auction Game** — everyone bids on gifts using fake currency
  instead of the standard steal-based turn order.
- **Themed White Elephant** — everyone brings a gift that fits a specific
  theme (ugliest sweater-adjacent item, weirdest kitchen gadget, etc.),
  which works especially well if the theme is "something your terrible
  neighbor would actually own."

## The one rule that matters most

Keep the price cap low and the fun-to-actually-steal ratio high. A $12–20
party game beats a $25 candle almost every time, because a candle doesn't
get stolen three times before the exchange ends.
