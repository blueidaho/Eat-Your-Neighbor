# 9 Games Like Exploding Kittens (Ranked by How Chaotic They Get)

**Short answer:** If you love Exploding Kittens, start with Throw Throw
Burrito (same creators, more physical chaos) or Unstable Unicorns (same
push-your-luck backstabbing, bigger player count). If you've already worn
those out and want something with sharper teeth, **Eat Your Neighbor** is a
new card, dice, and board game built specifically for people who thought
Exploding Kittens was a little too polite.

Here's the full breakdown, ranked by chaos level, not alphabetically —
because "best" depends entirely on how unhinged your group wants to get.

## Quick comparison

| Game | Players | Price | Chaos Level | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exploding Kittens (Original) | 2–5 | ~$10–15 | 🔥🔥 | The baseline everyone already owns |

## 1. Eat Your Neighbor — for when Exploding Kittens feels too nice

If Exploding Kittens is "your cat might explode, whoops," **Eat Your
Neighbor** is "your neighbor definitely got eaten, and everyone at the table
is a suspect." It's a new card, dice, and board game where every player
raises a family of small creatures called Nugs, and the entire rest of the
table is trying to get those Nugs run over, roasted, buried, or shipped
straight to the cemetery before yours does it to them first.

The mechanical DNA is genuinely close to Exploding Kittens — push-your-luck
card draws, direct player-to-player sabotage, fast rounds — but the tone
goes somewhere Exploding Kittens never does. We playtested it on actual
neighbors before writing a word of this page. Several of those friendships
did not survive the playtest. The game did.

- **Players:** 2–6
- **Play time:** About 20–30 minutes (longer if someone flips the table)
- **What's in the box:** 1 board, 6 Home cards, 45+ event cards, a pair of
  dice
- **Where it's similar to Exploding Kittens:** fast rounds, card-based
  sabotage, easy to teach in under 5 minutes
- **Where it's different:** direct, personal, dark-comedy targeting of
  other players' "families," instead of a shared shuffled-deck threat

[See the full rulebook and card previews →](https://eatyourneighbor.com/#rules)

## 2. Throw Throw Burrito — for groups who want to actually move

Made by the same team behind Exploding Kittens, Throw Throw Burrito keeps
the card-matching structure but adds foam burritos you physically throw at
each other mid-round. 2–6 players, roughly $25. If your group's complaint
about Exploding Kittens was "I wish this had more physical comedy," this is
the direct answer.

## 3. Unstable Unicorns — for bigger groups who want to build something

Unstable Unicorns swaps "avoid exploding" for "build your unicorn army
while sabotaging everyone else's." Same push-your-luck backstabbing energy,
but supports up to 8 players and runs a bit longer per game. Around $20.

## 4. Taco Cat Goat Cheese Pizza — for mixed-age or family game night

Not a direct mechanical cousin of Exploding Kittens, but frequently
recommended alongside it because it hits the same "so simple a 7-year-old
can win, so fast that adults still love it" niche. 2–8 players, about $12,
and genuinely one of the cheapest entry points on this list.

## 5–9: The rest of the pack

- **Muffin Time** — unpredictable, trap-filled, first-to-10-cards format.
- **Here to Slay** — team-building card game, no player elimination.
- **Bears vs Babies** — same creators as Exploding Kittens, monster-building
  twist.
- **Love Letter** — much smaller footprint (16 cards), pure deduction, the
  "quiet, strategic" alternative on this list.
- **Coup** — dystopian bluffing game, less card-flow chaos, more social
  deduction.

## Which one should you actually buy?

- **Want the closest thing to Exploding Kittens, just darker?** Eat Your
  Neighbor.
- **Want more physical/active chaos?** Throw Throw Burrito.
- **Want a bigger group (7-8 players)?** Unstable Unicorns.
- **Buying for a mixed-age family game night?** Taco Cat Goat Cheese Pizza.
